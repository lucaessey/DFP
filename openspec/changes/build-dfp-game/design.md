# Design

## Context
Empty Windows workspace, Node 24, no prior source or saved progress. See proposal.md for scope and explicit user decisions. OpenSpec 1.13.1 is project-pinned. Production assets must have no network dependencies.

## Goals / Non-Goals
Goals: compact deterministic simulation, legible original isometric art, meaningful direct interaction, reliable local persistence, independently verifiable business rules.
Non-goals: backend, network accounts, cloud sync, multiplayer, real-money economy, closed-app earnings, deployment, or archiving this change.

## Decisions
1. Native ES modules, Vite and pinned Three.js. Actual rounded 3D geometry with an orthographic elevated three-quarter camera, a hemisphere light and one shadow-casting directional light. Reuse geometries/materials, merge static furniture by material, and bound particles and pixel ratio. Procedural meshes and local lettering need no external assets. Floor-specific furniture, colored trims, low walls, legged tables, doorways and grounded characters establish scale. The camera follows smoothly within clamped kitchen/dining bounds; reduced-motion mode uses steady area views that switch when moving into the other wing.
2. `config.js` owns balance, layouts, and cosmetics. `simulation.js` owns all state transitions and fixed 0.05-second ticks. Rendering/UI/audio/storage import rules, never determine income. A seeded generator controls arrivals/orders. Persist its seed and all action timers. Runtime rendering interpolates but cannot award currency.
3. Model worker work as goals at interaction pads around solid furniture. Use a shared grid pathfinder for employees and tap-to-walk; keyboard/joystick use collision-aware motion. All meaningful stations are accessible. Customer state machines animate entrance, queue, service, play/dining, and departure.
4. Takeout: prep raw controllers, fry from raw stock, collect finished meals/drinks, unload at the separate STACK FOOD pad, then stand in the middle SERVE circle to hand out stacked goods and collect completed payment. Per the user's follow-up, direct hand delivery at SERVE is prohibited; counter quantities persist in saves and visible piles. Dining: collect green tower/red-blue handheld meals and wine, stack at the counter, serve and collect payment in the middle ring, then seat at purchased tables, eat, leave and clean. Shop: stockroom to shelves to browsing customer to checkout. Arcade: customers queue/use machines, leave quarters, workers collect; VR is a separate timed lane-dodge overlay. Each floor has a labeled trash can: only the player discards one carried item per action interval, with no refund.
5. Employee AI selects unmet work from current floor state. Multiple workers share stock/customer state atomically; each tick re-evaluates goals. Transfer returns undelivered items, cancels personal timers/path, and leaves table/customer state intact. Profit is applied once on final collection using the stated additive formula. Employee cap recommendation: three levels per category.
6. Save the complete state after discrete purchases/collections and regularly during movement. Store validated snapshots in primary and backup localStorage keys `dfp.save`/`dfp.backup`, with checksum envelopes, version migration, and safe recovery. Reject unknown future saves and disable writes, allowing export. No wall-clock catch-up. Visibility pauses the clock. Prevent simultaneous-tab writers with a browser lock when available and show a read-only notice in the second tab.
7. Responsive cream/ink/orange UI, four bottom tabs, desktop side cards and mobile compact HUD. WebGL canvas fills the gameplay area; projected DOM station buttons provide readable labels and comfortable touch targets. Settings include sound, reduced motion, reduced effects, installation, local-save export, and update action. Outfit previews use the same 3D models as the game. Audio is synthesized locally.
8. Build plugin generates a revisioned service-worker precache from output assets. Install is atomic; a waiting worker activates only on user update. Cache-first hashed assets and navigation fallback work offline. Retain old version caches so old clients remain functional. Never clear localStorage on update.

## Adjustable initial balance
Starting cash $120; takeout meal $1; drink +$3; dining $6 plus wine $4; souvenir $5; keychain $3; arcade 3 quarters/session; VR $3 plus $1 per dodge; floor unlocks $650/$1,600/$3,000. These low denominations follow the user's latest explicit payout request. First hire $100 then increasing by origin floor/index. Player upgrades start at $90 and increase with the used allowance. Production/interactions are roughly 0.5–3 seconds, dining/play 5–8 seconds. Player speed +15% per level, carry base three plus one per level; worker speed +20%, capacity base two plus one. Values reside in configuration.

Every offering requires an unlock: controller $50, drink $180; tower $140, handheld $180, wine $220; souvenir $180, keychains $500; arcade cabinets $180/$240/$300, VR $850. All begin locked on new saves. Preserve $50 from optional spending until controller is opened to prevent a new-game dead end. Customer demand and production are gated by product ownership. Existing available products are grandfathered during migration. Lower payouts implement the user's explicit request for harder earnings without lengthening movement or interaction delays.

User follow-up balance adjustment: after unlocking drinks, 80% of incoming takeout orders include a drink (previously 50%). Apply the same chance to untouched waiting orders on unlock; preserve orders already partially delivered. The value is `drinkOrderChance` in configuration.

## Risks / Trade-offs
- World label readability on phones → brief projected DOM station labels, active-station details and accessible controls.
- Complex worker paths → shared collision map and automated long-running full-floor service tests.
- Storage unavailable/full → explicit warning, backup/export, no claim of saved state after errors.
- Local saves can be edited → accepted for a single-player local game; validation protects stability rather than anti-cheat.
- Physical iOS/Android installation and platform interruption cannot be fully reproduced in desktop Chromium → document unavailable device tests separately.

## Migration Plan
The 3D presentation adds an optional boolean reducedEffects preference defaulting to false; the expanded seating release writes schema version 4 as described below. Models, animation phases, crowd offsets, particles and camera state are transient. Presentation reads simulation state and never triggers transactions. Renderer recreation, floor switching or context restoration cannot replay money or consume inventory. System reduced motion is honored for new games. Visual crowd separation stays within walkable space and does not change production timing.

The current release writes schema version 4 (the initial product-unlock release used version 3). Accept legacy v1 snapshots by merging newly introduced defaults only after checking core fields, then validate the complete result. Earlier v2 snapshots without the counter-stacking addition gain empty counters; completed old tutorials advance to the completed five-step tutorial. Version 2 migrates to version 3 with product ownership derived from already-open floors and sections. Unsupported future snapshots are preserved. Build updates replace code caches only. Keep this OpenSpec change active and unarchived. The subsequent user request authorizes publication to GitHub Pages at lucaessey.github.io/DFP/.

## Public hosting
Keep the ordinary Vite build at the root for localhost. A separate build:pages command uses base /DFP/ and dist-pages. Derive precache URLs and offline navigation from Vite's resolved base; register the worker under that same scope. Relative manifest identity, scope, start URL and icon paths support both builds. GitHub Actions runs the simulation tests before building and uploading only the compiled game. No credentials, development tools, test outputs or local saves are deployed. Publication verification uses isolated browser profiles, including offline reload and phone input; public-origin progress remains separate from localhost.

## Presentation verification
First verify floor one walking, preparation, carrying, stacking, serving, payment and employee work. Extend the same rig and interaction system to dining, shopping and arcade. Compare the same saved fixture before and after; record a real-time gameplay clip. Measure actual frame intervals, render cost, draw calls and triangles in the available browser, including reduced effects and a crowded scene. Target 60 FPS where practical without claiming physical phone results. Validate save compatibility, motion/effects settings, touch controls, all floor lifecycles, context recovery and complete production offline caching.

## Subsequent authorized service and seating revision
The user's September 23 instruction supersedes the initial dining service lifecycle. Every floor gains STACK FOOD and a distinct middle SERVE pad. All food is unloaded onto the counter before serving, and payment happens there before seating. Dining retains its console meals and wine. Gift merchandise and arcade machines/quarters/VR remain intact; those floors additionally sell separately unlocked controller snacks ($3 and $4 respectively).

Expand the shared world to 22 by 14 units with a dedicated dining wing containing six purchasable tables per floor. First-table prices are $60/$100/$140/$180, with $55 added per subsequent table. No tables are granted on new games. Without owned tables food is takeaway; with owned tables, paid diners wait for a free clean seat. Table states progress free → reserved → occupied → dirty → free. Dirty begins only when the eating timer finishes, never at service or seating. Manual or employee cleanup is required. Table ownership, reservation and eating timers persist. Cleaning has no earnings and cannot affect an occupied table.

Schema four extends counters/stocks and customer purposes/states. Preserve all schema-three progress, add locked snack products and unowned seating, and grandfather the two existing dining-floor tables. Previously unfinished dining orders return to the service queue retaining delivered items and paid status, so already-delivered food is never consumed twice. Existing dirty tables remain dirty. Larger room geometry and camera follow preserve the 3D rig and offline asset strategy.

## Orders of one to three items
Generate one to three requested units from unlocked offerings, allowing repeats. Food orders keep at least one meal; unlocked drinks appear in about 80% of takeout and dining orders and count toward the three-item maximum. Untouched drink-unlock refreshes replace the final unit when an order is already full. Existing per-unit delivery flags represent partial service and save without a schema change. Merchandise browsing advances through each missing item, visibly carries the complete basket, and sums all units at checkout. Workers prioritize older unmet shop demand against snack demand so longer food orders cannot indefinitely delay shelf restocking.
